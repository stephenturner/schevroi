About the data

The figures are SCHEV's (State Council of Higher Education for Virginia), copied from its Return on Investment Report in October 2026, one institution at a time. They cover bachelor's programs. SCHEV's estimates draw on the 2018-19 graduating cohort and on about 25 years of person-level Virginia wage records ending in 2019-20, adjusted to 2023 dollars.

The dashboard's institution list had 49 entries. This copy has 39 institutions and 726 programs, so some institutions are missing, and we have not confirmed why. Use the original dashboard as the authoritative source.

Read with care

These are estimates for graduates who stayed and worked in Virginia, and for some programs fewer than a third of graduates have wage records. Differences between schools also reflect who enrolls and where graduates live, so the numbers are not program rankings.

Who is not counted

  • Graduates who left Virginia or never worked in a job covered by Virginia unemployment insurance.
  • Federal employees, independent contractors, commission-only agents, railroad employees, elected officials, some religious and nonprofit employees, and certain agricultural and domestic workers.
  • Graduates who went on to earn a higher degree. That understates ROI for majors that feed graduate and professional school, such as biology, chemistry, and psychology.

How SCHEV calculates it

For each program area, SCHEV projects lifetime wages to age 67 from historical earnings, using seven-year moving medians and a linear fit. It compares those wages with a counterfactual: the same students entering the workforce after high school and earning high school wages to age 67. Cost includes student loan debt and net price.

Net lifetime difference
Bachelor's lifetime wages minus high school lifetime wages, student debt, and net price. A positive value is an estimated net gain from the degree.
ROI ratio
Bachelor's lifetime wages divided by the sum of high school lifetime wages, student debt, and net price. Above 1 means the estimated gain covers the cost; below 1 means it does not.
Share with wage data
The percentage of a program's graduates matched to Virginia wage records. The other two numbers rest on this group only.

Reading the numbers

  • Location and personal characteristics can move earnings as much as the degree does. Programs whose graduates work in Northern Virginia look better partly because wages there are higher, and the cost of living is not subtracted.
  • The data has no occupation or industry, so it cannot show whether graduates work in their field.
  • Many bachelor's programs prepare students for graduate study rather than a specific job, unlike business, engineering, nursing, allied health, and teacher preparation.
  • Past earnings do not predict future ones. In March 2026 SCHEV began using ROI as one factor in reviewing public programs already flagged for possible discontinuation; ROI alone does not trigger a review.
  • William & Mary economist David Feldman told VPM the method is sound but should not be used to rank programs across schools. This page lists institutions alphabetically for that reason.
